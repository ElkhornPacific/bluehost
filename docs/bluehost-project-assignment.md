The Problem
Small business owners come to Bluehost to establish their online presence — but getting a site live is only the beginning. The harder problem is what comes after: staying visible, generating leads, and keeping up with the ongoing work of running a business online. Most SMB owners don't have a marketing team, an SEO specialist, or a dedicated support rep. They have a few hours a week, a limited budget, and a business to run. 

AI agents represent a real opportunity to close that gap — acting as always-on digital employees that handle repeatable, high-value tasks on the customer's behalf. But there's a problem: for the average small business owner, "AI agent" sounds complicated, risky, and abstract. If the experience of discovering and activating an agent feels like configuring software, most customers will ignore it entirely. 

We have existing Bluehost customers — people who already have a website — who could benefit immediately from AI agents. They're logged in, they're active, and they have real business problems we can solve. But we haven't yet created a clear, trustworthy path from "I didn't know this existed" to "this is working for my business." 

The friction we know exists: 
•	Customers don't know what agents are available or which one is relevant to them 
•	Agent capabilities are described in technical or feature-led language, not business outcome language 
•	There's no clear setup moment that feels safe — customers worry about what the agent will do without them 
•	There's no feedback loop showing that an agent is doing something valuable 

The Ask
 Design the AI agent experience for an existing Bluehost customer — from the moment they first discover that agents exist, through activating one, to see that it's working for their business. 
You choose which agent to focus on. Pick one that solves a problem common to small businesses managing their online presence — something most Bluehost customers would recognize as painful and valuable. The agent you choose, and why, is part of what we're evaluating. 
Your solution should address: 
1.	Discovery — How does an existing customer find out that AI agents are available, and how do you make the right one feel obviously relevant to them? 
2.	Setup — What does activating an agent look like? How do you make a non-technical customer feel confident, not overwhelmed? 
3.	Ongoing value — How does the customer know the agent is working? What do they see, and how does it connect to outcomes they care about? 
You are free to define the scope further — that's part of what we're evaluating. 
Constraints: 
•	Scope to the Bluehost Portal. Do not design the sign-up or checkout flow. 
•	The customer is not technical. They understand their business, not software. 
•	Focus on one agent type. Depth over breadth. 

What to Deliver
1.	A clickable prototype — use any AI-assisted tool of your choice (Lovable, v0, Figma, or similar). Share it as a live link. Static mockups are not sufficient. 
2.	A short written brief — 1 to 2 pages covering: 
a.	The customer problem and why it matters 
b.	Which agent you chose, why, and what you prioritized or cut 
c.	How you handled the trust and simplicity challenge for a non-technical user 
d.	Tradeoffs you made and what you'd do differently with more time or data 
e.	2–3 metrics you'd use to measure success post-launch 

A Note on AI Use
We expect you to use AI tools to build the prototype — that is intentional. We are not evaluating whether you can hand-code a UI. We are evaluating how effectively you use AI to move from problem to prototype quickly, and how much product thinking you bring to what you build.
